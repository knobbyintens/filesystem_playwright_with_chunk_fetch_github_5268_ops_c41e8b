Line summaries

Copper Line (CL)

The Copper Line was conceived in the 1960s as an east-west distributor linking the western wharves with the growing eastern suburbs. Construction proceeded in three phases: the western trunk opened first, followed by the central section through Guildhall Cross, and finally the eastern extension to Opal Gardens. The one-stop Silverbirch spur was added later to serve a redeveloped industrial estate. The line takes its name from the copper works that once dominated the area around Copper Yard. Rolling stock was replaced in the 2010s with four-car trains featuring regenerative braking. The line is controlled from the Meridian Central signal box, with local control desks at Northgate Depot. Engineering possessions are normally taken on Saturday nights, when demand is lowest. The Copper Line carries approximately 96,000 passengers on an average weekday.

Stations: 16. Service pattern: Six trains per hour in each direction on the trunk; a shuttle service operates on the Silverbirch spur. Orientation: Sequence numbers increase towards the east; Eastbound services travel from lower to higher sequence numbers and Westbound services travel from higher to lower numbers. The Silverbirch spur is numbered after Kestrel Park.

Central Line (CT)

The Central Line follows the historic north-south tram corridor and was converted to metro operation in stages during the 1970s and 1980s. Its alignment passes through the civic centre, the university quarter and the northern residential districts. Larkspur Junction is the line's principal interchange and is served by Central Line trains only; Circle Line services connect by a short walking link. The line was resignalled in 2018 and now operates with automatic train protection throughout. Night services are limited to the southern section during engineering works.

Stations: 15. Service pattern: Eight trains per hour in each direction. Orientation: Sequence numbers increase from south to north.

Circle Line (CR)

The Circle Line is a single-direction loop encircling the eastern commercial district. It was built in the 1990s on former goods-yard alignments and connects the docklands with the retail core. The loop is operated as a continuous circuit, with clockwise and anti-clockwise services alternating. Beacon Quay provides a walking interchange to the Harbour Line, and Kingsfold connects to the Central Line.

Stations: 12. Service pattern: Continuous clockwise and anti-clockwise service. Orientation: Circular line; sequence numbers run clockwise.

Harbour Line (HB)

The Harbour Line serves the waterfront and the coastal communities beyond the breakwater. It opened in phases between 1988 and 2006 as part of the docklands regeneration programme. The line is exposed to coastal weather and is subject to speed restrictions during high winds. Anchorhead provides the main interchange to the Circle Line.

Stations: 9. Service pattern: Four trains per hour in each direction. Orientation: Sequence numbers increase from the inner harbour towards the coast.

Engineering possession standards

The following standing instructions apply to all Copper Line possessions. They are reproduced here for planning convenience and do not replace the formal engineering rule book.

Clause 1.2: A possession may not commence until the protection officer has confirmed that the line is blocked to traffic and the worksite is earthed where required. The responsible supervisor must record the check in the possession workbook and notify the line controller using the standard read-back procedure. Any deviation requires a written variation approved by the engineering possession manager and the duty operations manager. Where the worksite crosses a station boundary, the station supervisor at each affected station must confirm local isolation and platform protection before work begins. Records are retained for a minimum of seven years and are audited quarterly by the safety team.

Clause 2.2: Station isolation certificates must be issued before any work on platform equipment within three metres of the running line. The responsible supervisor must record the check in the possession workbook and notify the line controller using the standard read-back procedure. Operational data exports

The illustrative diagram on this page is maintained by the communications team and is not used for operational sequencing. The line controller publishes an authoritative machine-readable route register at data/line-controller-export.json in the operations repository. The register records every station's line code, sequence number, previous station and next station, plus interchange notes. Any station sequence used in a possession plan must be reproduced from the register and validated against the published station count and the line's endpoints. If a page view of this document is clipped, open the saved full output or retrieve the register directly; do not infer station order from the positions in the schematic above.

Export schedule: the register is refreshed every Sunday at 02:00. Change requests should be raised with the line controller. The register is the only supported source for automatic possession planning.
